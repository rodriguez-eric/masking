WHEN THE OTHER EAR ANSWERS

Cross-hearing simulator

A test signal can reach the opposite cochlea through the skull. Interaural attenuation (IA) is the loss along that route. Masking the non-test ear helps make responses ear-specific.

The numbers below describe a hypothetical patient. The sound is a digital illustration of routing and attenuation, not actual bone conduction or a simulation of your hearing thresholds. dB HL values never control headphone output directly.

Fixed comfortable test-route tone; crossed amplitude = test amplitude × 10−IA/20. The isolated crossed route is not boosted. It may be inaudible at high IA. Illustrative noise is not a clinical masking prescription.

SIGNAL MEETS COMPETITION

Energetic masking

Noise can obscure a tone when their excitation patterns overlap in the auditory system. Keep the tone fixed and increase the noise by reducing SNR.

SNR

10 log₁₀(signal power / noise power)

Here SNR compares tone RMS with total broadband-noise RMS over the shared listening interval. It is not noise spectrum level or a predicted detection threshold.

Predict the lowest SNR at which you can still detect the tone. Keep your device and shared playback level unchanged.

03

THE ORDER MATTERS

Masking in time

Forward: noise ends before the tone. Backward: noise begins after the tone. Simultaneous (also called concurrent): noise overlaps the tone. Timing is scheduled on the audio clock.

Noise lasts 300 ms; the 1 kHz tone lasts 20 ms. Both include 5 ms onset/offset ramps. A zero gap means adjacent sounds, without overlap. In simultaneous mode the tone is centered within the noise, and gap is ignored.

Audibility varies. Forward masking is often easier to demonstrate than backward masking; failure to hear a difference is a valid observation. Non-simultaneous masking is not simply simultaneous energetic overlap.

MAKE A LISTENING DECISION

Find the hidden tone

Two intervals contain broadband noise. One also contains a 1 kHz tone. Choose the interval with the tone. Repeat at different SNRs and record what changes.

Listen for two 600 ms intervals, separated by 500 ms. Chance performance is 50%. This is a fixed-SNR exercise, not an adaptive clinical threshold test.

FROM AUDIBILITY TO UNDERSTANDING

Speech in noise

Listen to the supplied sentence recordings in calibrated babble at −3, 0, +3, +6, or +9 dB SNR. Explore one condition at a time, or compare two blocks of five unique sentences.

What this comparison means. Babble can mask through acoustic overlap (energetic masking) and interference from competing voices or linguistic content (informational masking). Changing SNR changes the overall challenge; it does not isolate informational masking. Isolating that mechanism requires additional controlled masker conditions, matched acoustics and an appropriate experimental design.

Settings lock during the comparison. Start the comparison before exploring to reserve all ten sentences. Exploration uses the same ten-sentence pool. Interrupted trials remain used; end the comparison if the pool runs out. Each sentence is used at most once across exploration and scored trials in this page session. After 10, reload for a new session. Seed + identical action sequence reproduces sentence order and noise offsets. Reversing block order across students helps assess order effects.
