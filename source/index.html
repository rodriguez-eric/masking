BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Your listening setup

This is a classroom demonstration, not a hearing test. Your results compare conditions on your own device.

1. Headphones on, volume low

Find a quiet spot. Start with low system volume. Turn off spatial audio, sound enhancement, and automatic volume adjustment if possible. Use the same headphones throughout.

A 1000-Hz tone with gentle noise, at the maximum tone level used here. Adjust system volume until comfortably soft. Stop if uncomfortable.

A question to take back to class

To estimate critical bandwidth, keep signal frequency and noise center frequency fixed, increase noise bandwidth, and hold noise spectrum level constant. This activity instead compares center frequencies at equal bandwidth and equal total digital RMS. It does not estimate critical bandwidth or isolate upward spread of masking.

Instructor guide & method

What students investigate

Detection of a fixed 1000-Hz tone in quiet and with 200-Hz-wide noise centered at 500, 1000, or 2000 Hz. The on-frequency noise should generally interfere most. The off-frequency comparison does not isolate upward spread because frequency spacing and headphone response differ.

Procedure

Three practice trials precede a quiet block and three randomized masker blocks. Each trial contains two 650-ms intervals separated by 450 ms. The 350-ms signal begins 150 ms into one randomly selected interval. Both intervals have the same masker condition. All signals use 20-ms onset/offset ramps. Sound is identical at both ears.

A two-down/one-up staircase targets approximately 70.7% correct under standard assumptions. Two consecutive correct responses lower the tone, while an error raises it. Steps are 4 dB until two reversals, then 2 dB. Each block stops after six reversals or 36 trials. The estimate averages the last four reversal levels, only when all six reversals occur. Limits are −66 to −12 relative dB. Bounds and incomplete runs are flagged.

Levels and synthesis

Noise is synthesized with an inverse FFT, complex Gaussian frequency bins, conjugate symmetry, and a 200-Hz rectangular passband. The finite FFT gives approximate band edges. Noise RMS is normalized to 0.04 before ramps. The fixed master gain is 0.18. Browser and hardware volume remain uncalibrated. The setup sound uses the maximum signal level, so students establish comfort before trials begin. No automatic output-gain changes occur.

Class plan

Allow 3 minutes for setup and prediction, 10–15 minutes for trials, and 5–10 minutes for discussion. Use the example-data route for students who do not listen. Collect CSVs through Brightspace. No results are sent to a server.
